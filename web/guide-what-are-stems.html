What are stems? A simple explanation

2 min read · Updated November 4, 2026

Quick answer

Stems are groups of instruments mixed together, usually vocals, drums, bass and other. They let you change the balance of a finished song. Open the splitter

If you have ever heard a producer talk about "pulling up the stems", you may have wondered what they meant. Stems are the building blocks of a finished song, and they are easier to understand than they sound.

A stem is a group of sounds

When a song is recorded, there can be dozens of individual tracks: kick, snare, hi-hats, bass, several guitars, keyboards, lead vocal, harmonies. Mixing all of them gives the song you hear. A stem is a smaller bundle of those tracks mixed together, such as "all the drums" or "all the vocals". Most songs are delivered as four stems.

The four common stems

  • Vocals: the lead singer and usually the backing vocals.
  • Drums: kick, snare, cymbals and percussion.
  • Bass: the bass guitar or synth bass.
  • Other: everything else, such as guitars, piano, strings and synthesisers.

Stems versus multitracks

A multitrack is the full set of original recordings. A stem is a combination of them. Multitracks give the most control but are huge, and are rarely shared. Stems are small enough to share and still let you change the balance of a song.

Why people want stems

  • Musicians mute their own instrument and play along with the rest of the band.
  • Singers practise with a backing track and no lead voice.
  • DJs and producers make remixes and mashups, with permission from the rights holders.
  • Teachers and students isolate the bass line or the drum pattern to study it.
  • Listeners are curious and want to hear what each part is doing.

Where do stems come from now?

Artists rarely release them, but AI separation models can estimate them from a finished stereo song. They are not identical to the studio stems, though for practice, study and creative play they are often more than good enough.

Try it

Pick a song you know well, split it and listen to each stem alone. Hearing the bass or the drums without anything else is one of the fastest ways to understand how a song works.
